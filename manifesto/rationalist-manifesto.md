# The Rationalist Manifesto

*A constitution for version-controlled government.*

---

## I. The premise

For three centuries we have governed through representation because we had no alternative.
A citizen in 1726 could not read a bill before Parliament, could not follow a debate in
another city, could not know what her government had decided until weeks after it decided
it. Representation was not a philosophical ideal; it was a bandwidth constraint. We sent a
few people to a distant room because the room could not hold everyone, and the news could
not travel fast enough to matter.

The constraint is gone. Every person can now be aware, in the moment, of every decision
made in their name. What remains is the machinery built for the old constraint: laws
drafted in private, amended in committee rooms, published as monolithic documents whose
history is a matter for archivists. The text of the law is public; the *process* of the
law — who changed which words, when, why, and who agreed — is effectively secret, not
because it is hidden but because it is unrecorded, scattered, and unreadable.

Software engineering solved this exact problem twenty years ago. No serious engineering
team would accept a codebase that changes without attribution, without review, without
history, without the ability to see precisely what changed between yesterday and today.
Yet this is how every nation on Earth maintains its most important codebase: the law.

**We propose that the state be placed under version control.**

## II. The five principles

### 1. The law is a repository

Every constitution, statute, act, regulation and policy exists as plain, readable text in
a public **codex** — a governed body of related law. The codex is the ground truth. If it
is not in the codex, it is not the law. Related instruments live together; distinct
domains live apart. The full state of the law at any moment in history is reconstructible
to the character.

### 2. Every change is an inscription

No word of law changes without an **inscription**: an immutable record naming the change,
its author, its sponsors, its time, and its justification. Inscriptions are permanent.
History is never rewritten, only extended. A law's past is as public as its present —
including every draft that failed, every amendment that was voted down, and everyone who
voted it down.

### 3. Change moves through chambers

A proposed change is a **motion**. A motion is a small, legible amendment — never an
omnibus. It moves through **chambers**: stages of scrutiny, each a public branch of the
codex. In the United Kingdom these map naturally: a `commons` chamber, a `lords` chamber,
an `assent` chamber, and the `statute` branch — the official, in-force text. Other
polities will define their own chambers; the principle is invariant: **no change reaches
the statute branch without passing every chamber in the open.**

Small amendments are not a stylistic preference; they are the security model. A
thousand-page omnibus bill is the legislative equivalent of a ten-thousand-file commit —
unreviewable by design, and therefore a vehicle for the unreviewed. When change arrives
in small motions, the incentive to smuggle collapses, and the relevant experts and the
affected public can actually read what they are assenting to.

### 4. Every citizen may petition, witness and vote

Any citizen may open a **petition** against any codex — a request for change, a report of
harm, a question that demands an answer. Petitions that gather support cross thresholds
that create obligations: at the first threshold, the custodians must respond; at the
second, the legislature must debate and either open a motion or publish its reasons for
declining. Petitions are answered with motions, not press releases: if the state acts, the
act is a visible amendment traceable back to the petition that demanded it.

Citizens **witness** the codices they care about and cast **assent** — aye or nay — on
motions in the chambers open to them. Representation does not vanish; it becomes
accountable. A representative's every vote, every sponsorship, every amendment is a
permanent public record attached to their name.

### 5. The state is measured

A government that cannot be measured cannot be judged. Every polity maintains an
**observatory**: the vital signs of the nation — growth, migration, trade, health,
education, inequality, safety, environment, human development — published continuously,
sourced transparently, and comparable with every other polity on Earth. The observatory
is not a press office. Its figures carry their provenance, their reference periods, and
their revisions, permanently.

## III. What this replaces

We do not propose to abolish parliaments. We propose to abolish their opacity.

The chamber system preserves deliberation, expertise and delay — the genuine virtues of
representative institutions — while removing what was never a virtue: the illegibility of
the process to the people it governs. Under version control:

- **Provenance replaces rumour.** "Who put that clause in?" has an exact, permanent answer.
- **Amendments replace omnibus bills.** Change arrives in reviewable units, or it does not arrive.
- **Petitions replace lobbying asymmetry.** The channel from citizen to legislature is public
  infrastructure, not private access.
- **The observatory replaces the press release.** Performance is a dataset, not a narrative.
- **History replaces amnesia.** Every failed proposal, every reversal, every vote survives.

## IV. The transition

No revolution is required. The transition is an act of *transcription*, and it has already
begun in this repository:

1. **Transcribe.** Each polity's constitutional instruments and statutes are transcribed
   into codices, with their true history reconstructed — original sponsors, readings,
   assents, amendments and repeals — from the public record.
2. **Mirror.** New legislation is mirrored into the codices as it moves, in parallel with
   existing procedure. The codex becomes the most legible place to follow the law, then
   the default place, then the authoritative place.
3. **Bind.** Legislatures adopt the codex as the instrument of record: bills are drafted
   as motions, debated against amendments, enacted by merge into the statute branch.
4. **Open.** Petition thresholds acquire legal force. The channel from citizen to statute
   is complete.

The United Kingdom is the first polity transcribed here — fitting, for the country that
invented parliamentary government and never wrote its constitution down in one place. Its
constitution is famously "uncodified". Very well: **we shall codify the process instead.**

## V. The claim

We are called rationalists because we make a modest, testable claim: **that a state whose
every change is visible, attributed, reviewable and reversible will govern better than one
whose changes are not** — that sunlight is not merely a disinfectant but a compiler, and
that a nation which can read its own source code will, over time, debug itself.

Every decision visible. Every change attributed. Every citizen a reviewer.

*This manifesto is itself a document under version control. Propose a motion.*
