# Northern Ireland Act 1998

A peace treaty compiled into statute: the legal form of the Belfast (Good Friday)
Agreement, approved by referendum on both sides of the border.

## What it changed

- **The Assembly** — 90 members (originally 108) elected by single transferable vote,
  with legislative power over transferred matters.
- **Power-sharing by design** — a First Minister and deputy First Minister elected
  jointly; key decisions requiring **cross-community consent** (parallel consent of
  designated unionists and nationalists). Majoritarianism is structurally excluded.
- **The constitutional guarantee (s. 1)** — Northern Ireland remains part of the
  United Kingdom for as long as a majority of its people so consent, with provision
  for a border poll; a change in status requires the consent of both jurisdictions.
- **Rights and equality machinery** — the Northern Ireland Human Rights Commission and
  Equality Commission, born of the Agreement.

## Chamber record

The Agreement was signed on 10 April 1998, put to the peoples of Northern Ireland
(71.1% Yes) and the Republic of Ireland (94.4% Yes) on 22 May 1998, and enacted at
Westminster with Royal Assent on 19 November 1998 — the same day as the Scotland Act.
The settlement's history since — suspensions, St Andrews, restoration — is inscribed
rather than erased: the record of a constitution that has to be continuously
negotiated is itself part of the constitution.
