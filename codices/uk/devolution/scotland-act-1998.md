# Scotland Act 1998

The re-establishment, after 291 years, of a Scottish legislature — authorised first by
the people in a referendum, then by Parliament.

## What it changed

- **A Scottish Parliament** — 129 members elected by a mixed-member proportional
  system, legislating for Scotland in everything not expressly **reserved** to
  Westminster (defence, foreign affairs, the constitution, most macro-economics).
  The reserved-powers model means the default is devolved.
- **A Scottish Executive** — now the Scottish Government, headed by a First Minister.
- **Legislative consent** — the convention that Westminster "will not normally
  legislate with regard to devolved matters" without the Scottish Parliament's
  consent, made statutory in 2016.
- **Judicial policing of competence** — the courts, ultimately the UK Supreme Court,
  decide whether an Act of the Scottish Parliament is within its powers.

## Chamber record

The referendum of 11 September 1997 preceded the Bill — assent of the people recorded
before first reading, an ordering this platform regards as exemplary. Donald Dewar
introduced the Bill that December; Royal Assent followed on 19 November 1998 and the
Parliament assumed its powers on 1 July 1999. The settlement has since been twice
extended (2012, 2016), each amendment traceable to a published commission or
referendum commitment.
