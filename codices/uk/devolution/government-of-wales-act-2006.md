# Government of Wales Act 2006

The settlement that grew: Welsh devolution rebuilt as an institution capable of
becoming a parliament — and which then, step by inscribed step, became one.

## What it changed

- **Separation of powers** — the corporate body created in 1998 (assembly and
  executive fused) was split into a legislature and a Welsh Assembly Government.
- **A staged path to primary powers** — the Act contained its own upgrade mechanism:
  full law-making powers would activate if approved by referendum, which the people
  of Wales gave on 3 March 2011 (63.5% Yes).
- **Subsequent evolution** — the Wales Act 2017 moved Wales to a reserved-powers
  model and declared the institutions permanent; in 2020 the institution renamed
  itself **Senedd Cymru / Welsh Parliament** and enfranchised 16-year-olds.

## Chamber record

Introduced by Peter Hain in December 2005, Royal Assent 25 July 2006. Welsh devolution
is the platform's best example of *incremental constitutional change done in the open*:
1998 (executive assembly) → 2006 (separation, upgrade path) → 2011 (referendum
activates primary powers) → 2017 (reserved powers, permanence) → 2020 (self-renaming,
franchise extension). Five motions over twenty-two years, each small enough to read,
each building on the inscribed record of the last.
