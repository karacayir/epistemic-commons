# Rights & Liberties

If the Constitutional Instruments codex records what the state may not do to Parliament,
this codex records what the state may not do to *you*. Its four statutes form the
modern charter of the citizen: the rights you hold against public authorities, the
equality you are owed by employers and services, your right to know what the state
knows, and your rights over data about yourself.

## Documents in this codex

| Document | Citation | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Human Rights Act 1998 | 1998 c. 42 | In force |
| Freedom of Information Act 2000 | 2000 c. 36 | In force |
| Equality Act 2010 | 2010 c. 15 | In force (amended) |
| Data Protection Act 2018 | 2018 c. 12 | In force (amended) |

## Why these live together

Each of these Acts gives the individual an enforceable claim against power — in court
(Human Rights Act), in the workplace and marketplace (Equality Act), and against the
state's information asymmetry (Freedom of Information, Data Protection). They share
enforcement institutions (the courts, the Information Commissioner, the Equality and
Human Rights Commission) and are routinely litigated together.
