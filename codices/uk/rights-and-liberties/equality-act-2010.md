# Equality Act 2010

The great consolidation: one hundred and sixteen separate instruments — nine major
statutes and a thicket of regulations — merged into a single, legible codex of
discrimination law.

## What it changed

- **Nine protected characteristics** — age, disability, gender reassignment, marriage
  and civil partnership, pregnancy and maternity, race, religion or belief, sex, and
  sexual orientation — protected by one consistent set of concepts.
- **Unified definitions** — direct and indirect discrimination, harassment and
  victimisation defined once and applied everywhere: work, services, premises,
  education, associations.
- **Equal pay** — the sex equality clause implied into every employment contract,
  inherited from the Equal Pay Act 1970 and strengthened with gender pay gap reporting
  (added by regulations in 2017).
- **The public sector equality duty (s. 149)** — public bodies must consciously
  consider equality impacts in everything they do.
- **Reasonable adjustments** — the disability rights architecture of the DDA 1995,
  carried forward and extended.

## Chamber record

Introduced by Harriet Harman in April 2009, the Bill completed its passage in the
"wash-up" before the 2010 general election, receiving Royal Assent on 8 April 2010 with
front-bench support from all three major parties — an example of a large consolidation
motion enacted across party lines. As a merge of 116 instruments it is the exception
that proves this platform's rule: consolidation motions may be large, but only when
their purpose is to *reduce* the total complexity of the codex.
