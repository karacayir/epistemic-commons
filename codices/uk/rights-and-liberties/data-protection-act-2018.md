# Data Protection Act 2018

The citizen's rights over the state of being known: who may hold data about you, for
what purposes, and what you may demand they do with it.

## What it changed

- **A complete data protection code** — the Act implemented the GDPR's standards in UK
  law, added the law-enforcement and intelligence-services regimes, and replaced the
  Data Protection Act 1998.
- **Data subject rights** — access, rectification, erasure, portability and objection,
  enforceable against controllers public and private.
- **The Information Commissioner** — re-armed with fining powers of up to 4% of global
  turnover.
- **After exit** — since 2021 the Act operates alongside the "UK GDPR", the retained
  and domesticated regulation, making it the keystone of an independent UK regime.

## Chamber record

Introduced in the Lords in September 2017 and given Royal Assent on 23 May 2018 — 48
hours before the GDPR became applicable — the Act is an example of legislating against
a hard external deadline. It has since been substantively amended by the Data (Use and
Access) Act 2025 (Royal Assent 19 June 2025), whose passage this platform would have
recorded as a live motion with line-level amendments.
