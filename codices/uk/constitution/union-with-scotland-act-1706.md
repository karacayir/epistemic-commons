# Union with Scotland Act 1706

The founding merge of the British state: two sovereign parliaments ratify one treaty and
become a single polity.

## What it changed

The Act ratified the twenty-five articles of the Treaty of Union:

- **One kingdom** — England and Scotland united as Great Britain from 1 May 1707, under
  one Crown and the Hanoverian succession already fixed by the Act of Settlement.
- **One Parliament** — the Parliament of Great Britain at Westminster, with 45 Scottish
  MPs and 16 representative peers.
- **One market** — full freedom of trade and navigation, a common currency and unified
  customs and excise.
- **Two legal systems** — Scots private law, the Court of Session and the Church of
  Scotland were expressly preserved. The Union merged states, not laws: Scotland's
  separate legal system persists to this day.

## Chamber record

In this platform's terms, the Union was a two-chamber enactment across two polities:
the Parliament of Scotland ratified the Treaty on 16 January 1707 (Union with England
Act 1707), the Parliament of England on 6 March 1707 (this Act), and the merge took
effect on 1 May 1707. The Scottish Parliament's final act was, in effect, to approve
its own archival — until the Scotland Act 1998 re-established a Scottish legislature
291 years later.
