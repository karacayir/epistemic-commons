# Parliament Act 1949

The amendment that used the system to amend the system: the only major constitutional
statute enacted over the veto of the chamber it curtailed, by the procedure it was
curtailing.

## What it changed

A single, surgical amendment to the Parliament Act 1911: the Lords' power to delay a
public bill is reduced from **three sessions over two years** to **two sessions over
one year**. Nothing else. As a motion it is a model of the minimal amendment this
platform advocates — one clause, one purpose, fully legible.

## Chamber record

The Lords rejected the Bill in three successive sessions, so the Attlee government
enacted it under the 1911 Act's own procedure on 16 December 1949 — the 1911 Act
merging its own amendment over the Lords' objection.

Its validity was challenged 56 years later in *Jackson v Attorney General* [2005]
UKHL 56, on the argument that the 1949 Act was delegated legislation that could not be
used to amend its parent. Nine Law Lords unanimously disagreed: Acts passed under the
Parliament Acts are primary legislation. The Hunting Act 2004, enacted under the same
procedure, therefore stood.
