# Act of Settlement 1701

The statute that decides who wears the Crown — and, almost incidentally, the statute
that made judges independent of the Crown.

## What it changed

- **Succession settled by Parliament.** The Crown was settled on Electress Sophia of
  Hanover and "the heirs of her body being Protestants". Monarchs must be in communion
  with the Church of England. The message beneath the genealogy: the Crown is a
  creature of statute, and Parliament can rewrite the succession.
- **Judicial independence.** Judges' commissions were placed *quamdiu se bene
  gesserint* — "during good behaviour" — with salaries "ascertained and established",
  removable only on the address of both Houses of Parliament. Before this, judges
  served at the King's pleasure and were dismissed for inconvenient rulings.
- **Ministerial accountability.** A royal pardon cannot be pleaded to bar an
  impeachment by the Commons.

## Chamber record

Passed by the Parliament of England with Royal Assent on 12 June 1701, the Act executed
flawlessly thirteen years later: on Queen Anne's death in 1714 the Crown passed over
dozens of Catholic claimants with superior hereditary titles to George I of Hanover.
Its religious and gender provisions stood for three centuries until the Succession to
the Crown Act 2013 — agreed by all sixteen Commonwealth realms — amended them.
