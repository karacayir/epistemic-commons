# Constitutional Instruments

The United Kingdom has no single constitutional document. Its constitution is an
accumulation: charters extracted from kings, settlements imposed after revolutions,
and statutes that rebalanced the chambers of Parliament itself. This codex gathers the
instruments generally recognised as constitutional bedrock.

These documents span 796 years. Between the sealing of Magna Carta in a meadow at
Runnymede (1215) and the Parliament Act 1949, the pattern repeats: a crisis, a demand
for limits on power, and a text. Each text here is transcribed with its true history —
who forced it, who assented, what later Parliaments kept and what they repealed.

## Documents in this codex

| Document | Citation | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |

## Why these live together

Each instrument in this codex answers the same question — *where does power stop?* —
and each has been amended or invoked by the others. The Parliament Acts modify the
procedure by which every other document here could be amended; the Bill of Rights and
the Act of Settlement together define the Crown that gives assent. They form one
dependency graph and therefore one codex.
