# Representation of the People Act 1918

The largest single expansion of democracy in British history: the Act that gave the
vote to all men over 21 and to women over 30 who met a property qualification —
enfranchising some 8.4 million women and 5.6 million men at a stroke.

## What it changed

- **Manhood suffrage** — all men over 21 (19 for those who had served in the war),
  with the old property qualifications abolished.
- **Votes for women** — women over 30 who occupied property (or whose husbands did)
  could vote in parliamentary elections for the first time.
- **One day, one register** — general elections held on a single day, with a uniform,
  state-funded electoral register.
- Alongside it, the Parliament (Qualification of Women) Act 1918 allowed women to
  stand as MPs — at 21, paradoxically younger than they could vote.

## Chamber record

The Act emerged from the Speaker's Conference of 1917, a deliberately cross-party
chamber convened to settle the franchise question that pre-war politics could not. The
Commons carried the women's suffrage clause 385–55 on 19 June 1917; Royal Assent
followed on 6 February 1918. In this platform's terms, the suffrage movements had kept
a petition open for over fifty years — Parliament finally answered it with a motion.
