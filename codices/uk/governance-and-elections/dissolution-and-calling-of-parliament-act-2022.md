# Dissolution and Calling of Parliament Act 2022

The revert commit: eleven years after fixing election dates, Parliament restores the
status quo ante — with one controversial addition.

## What it changed

- **Repeal** — the Fixed-term Parliaments Act 2011 is repealed in full.
- **Revival** — the royal prerogative to dissolve Parliament is revived as if the 2011
  Act "had never been enacted": the Prime Minister may once again request a
  dissolution at a time of their choosing.
- **The ouster clause (s. 3)** — courts may not question the exercise, or purported
  exercise, of the revived dissolution powers. A direct legislative response to the
  Supreme Court's prorogation judgment in *Miller/Cherry* [2019] UKSC 41.
- **Five-year maximum** — a Parliament still expires automatically five years after
  first meeting.

## Chamber record

Introduced in May 2021 by Chloe Smith under Michael Gove's sponsorship, Royal Assent
24 March 2022, first exercised for the July 2024 general election. In version-control
terms this is a documented revert: the repealed Act remains in the codex, the reasons
are inscribed, and the ouster clause stands as a flagged, contested line — precisely
the kind of change whose history a citizen should be able to read in one place.
