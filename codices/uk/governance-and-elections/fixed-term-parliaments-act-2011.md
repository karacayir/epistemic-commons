# Fixed-term Parliaments Act 2011

An eleven-year constitutional experiment: what happens when Britain fixes its election
dates. Answer: 2017–2019 happens, and the experiment is reverted.

## What it changed

- **Fixed five-year terms** — elections on the first Thursday in May every five years,
  removing the Prime Minister's power to choose the date.
- **Two early exits only** — a two-thirds vote of the Commons, or a lost confidence
  vote not cured within fourteen days.
- The royal prerogative of dissolution was abolished outright.

## Chamber record

A product of coalition arithmetic — neither partner could risk the other calling a
snap election — the Act worked once (2017) and then jammed: in autumn 2019 a
government with no majority could not obtain a two-thirds vote, and Parliament
escaped the deadlock by passing the Early Parliamentary General Election Act 2019 by
simple majority, proving the lock could always be picked by any Commons majority.
Both major parties pledged repeal in 2019, and the Dissolution and Calling of
Parliament Act 2022 obliged.

This document is retained in the codex as a **repealed** instrument: under version
control, failed experiments are not deleted — they are the most instructive part of
the history.
