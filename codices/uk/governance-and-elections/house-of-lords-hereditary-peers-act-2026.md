# House of Lords (Hereditary Peers) Act 2026

The close of a 115-year-old open motion: the last hereditary legislators leave the
House of Lords.

## What it changed

The Act removes the exception created by the House of Lords Act 1999 that allowed 92
hereditary peers to remain. From the end of the 2024–26 parliamentary session
(29 April 2026), no one sits in the House of Lords by virtue of a hereditary peerage.
The hereditary by-elections — with electorates occasionally smaller than a dinner
party — are abolished with it.

Twenty-nine former hereditary members were given life peerages (announced December
2025 and May 2026) so their service could continue on individual merit — the
distinction the Act draws being not against persons but against inheritance as a
qualification to legislate.

## Chamber record

Introduced in September 2024, the Bill endured a long passage — including protracted
ping-pong between the Houses in early 2026 — before Royal Assent on 18 March 2026.
The Parliament Act 1911's preamble had promised a chamber "constituted on a popular
instead of hereditary basis"; the 1999 Act made the down-payment; this Act completed
the principal sum 115 years after the promise was inscribed. Long-open motions can
still be enacted — provided the record survives long enough for someone to finish the
work.
