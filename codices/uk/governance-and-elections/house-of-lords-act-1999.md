# House of Lords Act 1999

The Act that ended — almost — seven centuries of law-making by inheritance.

## What it changed

One operative sentence: *"No-one shall be a member of the House of Lords by virtue of
a hereditary peerage."* At a stroke, 666 hereditary peers lost their seats, reducing
the House from around 1,330 members to about 670.

The famous compromise: the **Weatherill amendment** retained 92 hereditary peers,
chosen by internal ballot among themselves, "until the second stage of reform". The
by-elections that refilled these seats as their holders died — electorates sometimes
of three voters — became a celebrated constitutional curiosity.

## Chamber record

Led by Baroness Jay in the Lords and Margaret Beckett in the Commons, with Royal
Assent on 11 November 1999. The "temporary" exception of 92 peers lasted 27 years —
a reminder of why this platform records open motions permanently: the second stage of
reform promised in 1999 (and indeed in the Parliament Act 1911's preamble) was finally
delivered by the House of Lords (Hereditary Peers) Act 2026.
