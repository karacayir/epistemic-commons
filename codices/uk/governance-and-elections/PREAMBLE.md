# Governance & Elections

This codex holds the machinery: the statutes that decide who may vote, who may sit in
each House, how the senior judiciary is appointed, and how a Parliament ends. It is the
most actively amended codex in the United Kingdom polity — its newest document received
Royal Assent in March 2026.

## Documents in this codex

| Document | Citation | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Representation of the People Act 1918 | 7 & 8 Geo. 5 c. 64 | Largely superseded |
| Representation of the People (Equal Franchise) Act 1928 | 18 & 19 Geo. 5 c. 12 | Superseded (consolidated) |
| House of Lords Act 1999 | 1999 c. 34 | In force (amended) |
| Constitutional Reform Act 2005 | 2005 c. 4 | In force |
| Fixed-term Parliaments Act 2011 | 2011 c. 14 | Repealed |
| Dissolution and Calling of Parliament Act 2022 | 2022 c. 11 | In force |
| House of Lords (Hereditary Peers) Act 2026 | 2026 c. 12 | In force |

## Why these live together

Franchise, composition, judiciary, dissolution: four subsystems of one machine. The
documents amend and repeal one another in a visible chain — the 1928 Act completes the
1918 Act; the 2022 Act repeals the 2011 Act; the 2026 Act finishes what the 1999 Act
began. Reading them in sequence is reading the changelog of British democracy.
