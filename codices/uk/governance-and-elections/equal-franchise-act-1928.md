# Representation of the People (Equal Franchise) Act 1928

The amendment that completed 1918: votes for women at 21, on the same terms as men.

## What it changed

A short Act with one purpose — it removed the age difference and property
qualification that the 1918 Act had attached to women's votes. About five million
women were enfranchised, and women became a majority of the British electorate at the
1929 general election.

Millicent Fawcett, who had led the constitutional suffragists for half a century, sat
in the gallery to watch the Lords pass the Bill; Emmeline Pankhurst had died three
weeks before Royal Assent.

## Chamber record

Baldwin's government introduced the Bill through Home Secretary Sir William
Joynson-Hicks — previously an opponent of women's suffrage — and the second reading
passed 387–10. Royal Assent came on 2 July 1928. As a motion it is the model
follow-up amendment: the 1918 enactment shipped with a known inequality; the 1928
amendment removed it cleanly and was then absorbed into later consolidations of
electoral law.
