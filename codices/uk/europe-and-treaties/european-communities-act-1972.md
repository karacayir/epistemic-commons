# European Communities Act 1972

Twelve sections that rewired the British constitution: for forty-seven years, the
conduit through which an entire legal order flowed into the United Kingdom.

## What it changed

- **Section 2(1)** — rights and obligations arising under the EU Treaties took effect
  in UK law automatically, "without further enactment": a standing, self-updating
  incorporation.
- **Section 2(4)** — all legislation, past and future, took effect subject to directly
  applicable Community law; in *Factortame* the courts confirmed this meant even Acts
  of Parliament could be disapplied.
- In this platform's terms, the Act subscribed the UK statute branch to an external
  upstream: changes merged in Brussels propagated into domestic law without a fresh
  Westminster inscription — the constitutional novelty, and grievance, at the centre
  of the next forty years of debate.

## Chamber record

Negotiated by Geoffrey Rippon under Edward Heath, the Bill survived its second reading
by eight votes (309–301) with the Prime Minister's confidence staked on it, and
received Royal Assent on 17 October 1972. Membership was confirmed by referendum in
1975 (67.2% remain) and reversed by referendum in 2016 (51.9% leave). The Act was
repealed on exit day, 31 January 2020 — retained here, like every repealed
instrument, because half a century of law is built on its foundations.
