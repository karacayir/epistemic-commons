# European Union (Withdrawal) Act 2018

The largest single version-control operation ever performed on a statute book: cut the
upstream, snapshot forty-seven years of incorporated law, and keep the system running.

## What it changed

- **Repeal** — the European Communities Act 1972 is repealed on exit day.
- **The snapshot** — the entire body of EU law applying in the UK is copied into
  domestic law as **retained EU law**, so the statute book does not develop tens of
  thousands of holes overnight.
- **Correction powers** — ministers receive broad (and much-criticised) powers to
  amend the snapshot by regulation, to fix "deficiencies" arising from withdrawal.
- **Sovereignty restored, complexity retained** — supremacy of EU law ends for new
  legislation; the courts must untangle what the snapshot means for decades of
  precedent. The 2023 Act later renamed the corpus "assimilated law".

## Chamber record

Introduced by David Davis in July 2017, the Bill consumed the parliamentary session:
hundreds of hours of debate, over a thousand amendments, and repeated government
defeats in the Lords, before Royal Assent on 26 June 2018. In this platform's terms
it is the counter-example that proves the rule: a change of this magnitude, forced
through as one motion, generated years of correction motions — visible in this very
codex — that a more incremental history might have avoided.
