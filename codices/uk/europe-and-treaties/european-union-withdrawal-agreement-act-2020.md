# European Union (Withdrawal Agreement) Act 2020

The ratification: the treaty of departure written into domestic law, ending the
deadlock of a Parliament that had rejected every prior version.

## What it changed

- **Ratifies the Withdrawal Agreement** — citizens' rights, the financial settlement,
  the Northern Ireland Protocol, and the implementation period to 31 December 2020.
- **Amends the 2018 Act** — the snapshot of retained EU law is deferred to the end of
  the implementation period, keeping EU law applicable during 2020.
- **Citizens' rights** — the EU Settlement Scheme is anchored in statute for millions
  of EU citizens resident in the UK.

## Chamber record

The 2018 Agreement was rejected three times by the Commons — by 230 votes (the
largest government defeat in modern history), then 149, then 58 — a deadlock resolved
not within the chambers but by returning to the electorate in December 2019. The new
Parliament passed the Bill in days: introduced 19 December 2019, Royal Assent
23 January 2020, exit at 23:00 GMT on 31 January 2020. The sequence stands in this
codex as the clearest modern demonstration that when chambers deadlock, the citizens
are the chamber of last resort — the principle this platform builds into its
petition thresholds.
